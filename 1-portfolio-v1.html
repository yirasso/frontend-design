01Position

A screen is not a poster. It is a machine somebody operates under time pressure, with one hand.

Most product design is decoration applied after the hard thinking is finished. I would rather do the hard thinking, and I charge accordingly.

So I measure. Tap targets. Error rates. The seven seconds it took a support agent at Tender to find the refund button before we moved it. The beautiful part is the last ten percent and it only holds up if the other ninety is right underneath it.

Three or four projects a year. No retainers, no logo work, no dashboards that exist to impress an investor.

03How it actually goes
01

Sit with the operators

The first week is watching people use whatever exists now. No workshops, no personas, no affinity wall. A notebook and a stopwatch, and one question repeated until it stops being polite.

02

Draw the smallest version

Grey boxes for as long as I can stand it. If the flow does not work without colour, colour will not save it. Prototypes go in front of the same people from week one, on their own hardware, at their own desk.

03

Hand over something buildable

Tokens, states, redlines, focus order, the error copy. The file a front-end engineer opens on a Monday morning without needing to call me. That handover is the deliverable — the pictures are a by-product.
