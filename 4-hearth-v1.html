It burns.
It does not loop.

Hearth writes music in the room it is playing in — measuring the reverb, then composing against it, forever, without ever arriving back at a bar it has already played.

A loop is a promise that nothing else is going to happen. We would rather not make that promise.

On method

Most “endless” music is eight bars and a crossfade. You hear the seam by minute four and stop hearing the music by minute six. Hearth does not hold a library of stems. It holds a model of a room — its impulse response, its floor noise, the way a hard surface at three metres eats the top end — and it writes into that room in real time, one bar ahead of the listener, at about 12 ms of latency.

The result is not ambient wallpaper. It has structure, it has arguments with itself, and it will occasionally go quiet for ninety seconds because the room got loud. We think that is the interesting part.

How a room gets written into

01

Sweep

A twenty-second logarithmic sine sweep, once, at install. From the recording we take the impulse response, the noise floor, and the six frequencies the room refuses to give back.

02

Bias

Those measurements become a bias on the model’s output distribution. A room that swallows 4 kHz gets writing that lives above it. Nothing is equalised after the fact.

03

Burn

The engine runs one bar ahead and discards it if the room changes — a door opening, forty people arriving. Discarded bars are never re-used, which is the whole reason nothing repeats.
